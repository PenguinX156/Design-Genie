# Campaign Lab direction

## Product and users

Campaign tracking for a small creative studio. Leads and producers need a fast read on reach, engagement, risk, and the next review. Functional and moderately dense; trustworthy numbers with a creative identity.

## Three directions considered

1. **Editorial operations** — strong serif headlines, compact data, warm paper, forest green, one chart-like campaign list. Low motion and low performance cost. Best fit: creative confidence and fast scanning.
2. **Spatial signal room** — dark technical canvas, floating metrics, animated connections. Memorable but distracts from routine review and costs more to build and run.
3. **Campaign scrapbook** — colorful campaign imagery and collage. Expressive but would need real brand assets and could weaken cross-campaign comparison.

Selected: **Editorial operations**. The other two are less appropriate for a daily operations view with numeric comparison.

## Layout and hierarchy

Persistent dark rail for workspace context; wide greeting and one typographic emblem; border-based metric strip; ruled campaign rows with reach bars; contrasting milestone block. Mobile stacks the rail, metrics, and campaign details while keeping filter and status visible.

## Signature moments

- Oversized editorial greeting with a small geometric emblem.
- Campaign rows function as a compact visual performance index.

## Anti-patterns here

Repeated rounded stat cards, decorative gradients, and motion on every row would dilute the editorial direction. Cards and gradients are not universally forbidden.
